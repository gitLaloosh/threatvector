Intel reads the chat log that is still being written

Page 2026-11-12 · v2026.11.12.1

Why

  • The owner, 2026-11-11: "intel reports are not being read. killmails do show on the list but not chat log reports." The status line said "no lines yet" for over ten minutes.
  • Cause: two of his characters had a log of the same intel channel. His alt's started hours after his main's and then went quiet (the alt logged off); his main's kept logging. Intel watched the log that started last (your own only if it started within an hour of that), so it read the quiet one. Logging off and on (new files) made it work. The owner: "on it's own branch as soon as possible."

What changed

  • itScan: for each chosen channel, the log written to last (its last-modified time) is watched; your own character's while it was written within a minute of that (IT_LOG_MINE_MS); the one watched now stays when another is only 2 s newer (IT_LOG_SAME_MS: two characters in a channel get the same line at about the same moment).
  • Moving to another log (a relog, an alt's log going quiet) reads its last 256 KB (IT_SWITCH_READ), skips the lines the old log already gave (the same time, who and text: itLineKey, itLineMark), and treats lines older than the last look through the folder as history (shown within "forget reports after", no alert); newer ones are live reports. The first log of a channel is read as before.
  • Nothing else: no stored data, no request, nothing users see except reports arriving.

Checks

  • New intel_logs_check.py (intel_check's stand-in folder, which keeps each file's write time): the owner's case (the main's log watched and read, the status line moves on), then the main's log going quiet while the alt's is written (the page moves; a line both carry read once; the new one live). Mutants: the old pick (the log that started last) and the move without skipping what was read, both caught.

Lesson for future models

LESSON #158: of several files that each could be the live one, pick by when it was last written, not when it started; and when moving between them, skip what the old one already gave.
