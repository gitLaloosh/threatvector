The Pilot Dossier read by fights (and a probe read as a pilot)

Pilot AnalyzerSystem AnalyzerIntel 2026-11-15 · v2026.11.15.1 · build queue 049 · plan roadmap/dossier-fights-plan.md (its section 13 is the build review)

Why

The owner: a pilot in a large alliance that "sometimes has very large battles during a war" has "their newest 50 kills and 30 losses ... accounted for in two or three hulls over a weekend or a week. so you get nothing useful about the pilot when they're out roaming with a small gang between large alliance fleets." He asked for a proposal "with our lookup budgets in mind".

What changed

  • Read by fights. zKillboard's kill pages (200 kills each, newest first) are read until their kills make 15 fights, a page reaches over a year back, 3 pages are read, a page holds fewer than 200, or a page is over 20 MB of data. Page 1 is drawn at once; the older pages are read after it ("reading older kills…" in the heading line) and the dossier is redrawn once, keeping the size, the row and the scroll. A new pilot meanwhile stops the reading. Losses: one page. Both the past year's only; a pilot with nothing in the past year shows what was read, and the heading says "(none in the past year)" (the owner at the review: "your plan works").
  • Fights: two kills are one fight when they share 2 of the other players (1 when either had only 1 or 2 others) and are within 3 hours of each other; the pilot himself is left out. The heading reads "N kills in N fights, N losses, from – to", its hover what was read and why it stopped; under each row's kill count "N fights".
  • No ESI killmail reads for a pilot. zKillboard's list items are the killmails (its own code: the ESI killmail plus a zkb object), so the dossier, the System Analyzer's pilot rows, the Last kill line and the fits from their losses use them as they are: up to 80 ESI reads per pilot before, 0 now. fetchKillmail uses a whole list item everywhere (the System Analyzer's group and system lists too).
  • System Analyzer rows read all of their first pages (up to 200 kills and 200 losses, not 50 and 30), the same 2 zKillboard requests.
  • Trimmed on arrival, kept trimmed. A pilot's own kill and loss lists are cut down as a page arrives: a kill to the victim, their own entry, the players counted, the other players' hulls and up to 100 player ids (a 2,000-attacker structure kill: about 1.6 KB, was about 374 KB); a loss to the victim (its fit) and its 20 top attackers. Kept for the hour under t: + the list's address in the zkb store. The group and system lists stay whole (they read every attacker).
  • Stored data stays under 50 MB. Once per load at idle and after a dossier read: the zkb entries past their hour are deleted, then, over 50 MB (navigator.storage.estimate()), the killmails used longest ago until under 40 MB. Never item types, groups, organisations, settings or sounds.
  • Intel: a probe dragged in from D-Scan ("<system> <pilot> Sisters Core Scanner Probe") made the probe's last word the Probe frigate and the words before it a pilot. Every probe's name (scanner, survey and interdiction probes, from CCP's data through pyfa: INTEL_PROBES, written by tools/gen_intel_tables.py) is read first as the probes tag (the owner: "probes only for now").
  • The dossier info panel's first line, and METHODOLOGY.md's dossier, possible ship and system lines.

Stored data

New keys only, inside existing stores: t:<address> in zkb. The older page reads the plain addresses, so it never gets a trimmed list; its own entries stay as they were. No database version change. The cap deletes old killmails and expired zkb entries, which both pages read again from zKillboard or ESI when missing.

Checks

  • tools/page_checks/dossier_fights_check.py (new): made-up pilots on a stand-in zKillboard that answers with whole killmails, pages and a page over 20 MB; the fight rule; page 1 first; the System Analyzer rows; the cap and the older page; 7 mutants.
  • tools/page_checks/intel_probe_check.py (new): the probe lines and two mutants.
  • dossier_check, dossier_kills_check, dossier_pin_check: the new heading and notes.

Lesson for future models

LESSON #166: cutting stored data down for one reader breaks the others. The plan trimmed every list in the hour's store, including the System Analyzer's, and three of its features read every attacker of those killmails; a whole-item test would have taken the trimmed copies as killmails and counted wrong without an error. List every reader of a store, and what it reads, before trimming it; mark the trimmed form and keep it under its own key.
