Intel in one tab at a time

Page 2026-11-08 · v2026.11.08.3

Why

  • Build queue 026, Project doc roadmap/intel-one-tab-plan.md (the clickable prototype stays in the Project, the owner's choice at the review). The owner: "we really only want users to utilize a single tab to begin with. multiple tabs complicate things like INTEL requests". Every tab with Intel on ran its own reader of zKillboard's live feed at up to 5 requests a second: three tabs reach zKillboard's 15-a-second line (an hour's block for the address, LESSON #93), and alerts sounded once per tab.

What changed

  • One tab runs Intel. A Web Lock is held exactly while Intel runs. In another tab, Intel's card says "Intel is on in another tab." with Move Intel here, and the switch is greyed out (the owner: the button is the only way, so nobody takes Intel from another tab without meaning to). Move Intel here asks for the folder from the press itself (needed in a new tab since 025), then takes the lock; the old tab stops and says "Intel moved to another tab." with Move Intel back here. Closing the running tab: "The tab running Intel was closed, so Intel is off." Nothing starts by itself.
  • The mark in every tab: the other tabs' profile mark and Intel tab dot show the running tab's state (its title says it's from the other tab). Sent only when it changes. A tab shown again checks whether the running tab still holds the lock, so a crashed tab doesn't leave a stale mark.
  • Staggered catch-up (the owner: "Only after a gap"): catching up after a minute or more without kills (a computer waking up) waits a random 0–10 s first, so browsers on one address don't read back at the same moment. Turning Intel on reads at once, as before.
  • A stopped reader sends nothing more (LESSON #148): requests already waiting at the feed's gate went out after Intel stopped (the switch, and now a move): up to 5 more in a few seconds. Found by the new check counting each tab's requests after a move; such a request is now dropped.
  • The Intel map's "ResizeObserver loop" errors: the map redraws on the next frame after its box changes size, as the Intel example already did.
  • Intel's ⓘ panel: one sentence under Reloads (the panel's shown words were at their limit). Nothing new is kept in the browser.

Checks

  • New intel_one_tab_check.py: two pages in one browser context with the made-up Chatlogs folder, R2Z2 requests counted per page. It covers the line and greyed switch in the second tab, the mark from the other tab, a click on the greyed switch, Move Intel here (the folder asked from the press), only the new tab reading afterwards, the permission refused, one alert across both tabs, a repeated mark not redrawn, the running tab closed and crashed, the catch-up wait, no Web Locks, the map's resizes and 390 px.
  • Confirmed in Chromium before building (the plan had them UNVERIFIED): two pages in one context share the lock and the channel, also from a local file; steal makes the old holder's request reject with AbortError; a crashed tab's lock is released.
  • Mutants: 11, all caught (no lock; the switch enabled while another tab runs; the old tab not stopping; starting by itself when the other closes; redrawn on every message; no catch-up wait; the map redrawn inside its observer; a stopped reader's queued requests sent; no word when the running tab closes; the lock not checked when shown, both halves).
  • The page's other two resize observers (the fit list's pinned card, the damage chart): no loop error in seven checks that resize or draw fits, run on a copy that turns the error into a page error.
  • The checks picked from the changes since main (8 areas, 82 page checks, 25 min): one failure from this change, intel_guard_check (the new gate check sat before the stop's own error; fixed, the stop first), then rerun with the served-copy checks: 0 failed.
  • Page size: source 791,690 bytes gzipped (gzip -9); served 493,696, 306,304 under the 800,000-byte ceiling.

The owner's hand test

  • Two tabs: turn Intel on in one; the other says so and shows the mark. Move it; the first stops. Close the running tab; the other's mark goes off and nothing starts.
  • Sleep and wake with Intel running: it catches up a few seconds after waking.
  • Press Back to the page from another site with Intel running: does Intel carry on, and do other tabs still see it running? (Unverified: whether Chrome keeps a page holding a lock in its back/forward cache.)

Lesson for future models

LESSON #148: stopping a reader must also stop what it already queued at a gate. A flag checked between batches doesn't reach requests already waiting their turn; check after the wait, and test "it stopped" by counting requests after the stop.
