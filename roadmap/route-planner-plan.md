## What and why
- [stated] 2026-11-12: "find out if we can replicate or request a route specifically as the EVE client would build it given their set options (shortest, safest, etc.)"
- [stated] "I'd like the option to set it in game for convenience. seeing the route in the tool doesn't help much if i have to manually set 25+ systems to a route manually to match the tool's route."
- [stated] "what if we had a search box w/ predictive text like the in-game search on the map. once a system is selected from the search, user can right click and see options to set route using one either of the two methods we plan to allow. route settings are already captured in the intel settings panel ahead of time. right click gives you the option to do in-game route generation or the tool's method that sets waypoints for everything but allows them to utilize options like "avoid gate camps" and shit like that."
- [stated] "right click on a system in the map (outside of the search box) could also give the option to set destination. in my previous message i meant to say "set destination" instead of "set route"."

## Decisions (the owner's, 2026-11-12)
- D1 Two ways to set the destination: [stated] "both buttons". Their names: [stated] "(EVE's route) and (Threat Vector's route)" → **Set destination (EVE's route)** (one waypoint, the destination; EVE plans with the player's own autopilot settings, jump bridges included) and **Set destination (Threat Vector's route)** (every system of the tool's route as a waypoint, in order, so EVE follows it).
- D2 Where: a **search box with suggestions** on the Intel map, and a **right-click menu on any system** of the map (D1's two items + "Avoid this system" / "Stop avoiding"). Route settings live in **Intel settings** ([stated] "route settings are already captured in the intel settings panel ahead of time").
- D3 Phones: [stated] "you decide, i dont have a lot of experience navigating sites like this on a phone." → the session's decision: a pressed system's tooltip carries the same three buttons, 44 px each (no long press: nothing shows it exists).
- D4 No preview step: [stated] "users will know if they want to see the route, they just left click on the destination. this also means routes drawn when doing these clicks should be generated using intel settings panel." → the map's drawn route (hover dashed, pressed solid) and the tooltip's route list follow **Threat Vector's route**; the right-click sets the destination at once.
- D5 Avoid options: [stated] "all of those" → gate camps, bubbles, systems with reports, systems on my avoid list. Widened by Q2: the third option is "systems with reported hostiles, neutrals, kills or ESS" (within "forget reports after").
- D6 No clean route: [stated] "fewest flagged systems with a warning." → the route passes the fewest flagged systems, then the game's cost decides; the warning names them (tooltip, menu line, and the note after setting the destination). The destination and your own system never count.
- D7 Mockup: [stated] "yes".
- Session decisions (the owner can change them): jump numbers stay on stargates (Near You, alerts, Feed, kill range, "Jumps from you", Paths to you, the tooltip's "N jumps"); only the drawn route and the tooltip's route list follow Threat Vector's route, with "Threat Vector's route: N jumps (shortest M)" when they differ. Defaults: Prefer shorter, security penalty 50 (the game's default), all four avoid options on, the list empty. The third option's label: "systems with reported hostiles, neutrals, kills or ESS".

## Questions from the mockup
- Q1 **Detours.** DECIDED 2026-11-12: [stated] "keep my rule. they just have to turn off a setting for what to avoid if they want a shorter route. make them decide rather than assigning an arbitrary number to reported systems." → D6 stays: fewest flagged systems first, whatever the detour length (in the mockup's made-up Intel 0TYR-T is 3 jumps by gate and 13 by Threat Vector's route). No "counts as N jumps" rule; the mockup's comparison switch was removed. The tooltip's "(shortest M)" shows the cost of the detour; the Routes box's ⓘ says that turning an avoid option off gives shorter routes.
- Q2 **Which reports.** DECIDED 2026-11-12: [stated] "every report that has hostiles, neutrals, camps, bubbles, kills or ess." → every report Intel holds (not only within "Map: within"; the map's reach only limits what's drawn), and the third avoid option flags hostiles, neutrals, kills and ESS (camps and bubbles keep their own options). Friendly-only and clear reports flag nothing.

## Scope
1. **Threat Vector's route** (`rpPlan`): one search from your system over INTEL_JUMPS (the map table's `adj`), worked out again only when your system, the route settings or the flagged set changes (a key, like `itMapKey`). Order: fewest flagged systems on the way (D6), then the cost into each system, then fewest jumps. Cost (CCP's route guide, see Sources): Shorter 1 a jump; Safer and Less Secure with `p = exp(0.15 × penalty)`: into null (security ≤ 0.0) 2p; into low (< 0.45) p for Safer, 0.90 for Less Secure; into high 0.90 for Safer, p for Less Secure.
   - **Security band** (change 1a): INTEL_MAP.sec is rounded to hundredths (`tools/jump_table_update.py` line 143), which can move a system across 0.0 or 0.45. `jump_table_update.py` also writes a band per system (0 null, 1 low, 2 high) from CCP's unrounded `securityStatus` (2 bits, or 1 byte base64; measure the served size). `--check` covers it; a band change opens the same pull request.
   - Systems outside INTEL_JUMPS (wormhole space, Pochven's own gates) as today: no gate route, the menu's items off ("no gate route").
2. **The map** (`itMapRoute` follows Threat Vector's route; `itMapBfs` stays for `itm.D`). The tooltip adds "Threat Vector's route: N jumps (shortest M)" when they differ, "⚠ No route avoids all: passes X (camp), Y (hostiles, kills)" when flagged systems are on it, "on your avoid list", and on a wide screen "right-click: set destination". `itMapKey` adds Threat Vector's route key so a settings or flag change redraws.
3. **Search box** (top left of the map box; on a phone under the zoom row, full width, 44 px; "Hide all routes" moves below it): suggestions as you type from `it.names` (names starting with the text first, then containing it, 8 at most), each with its region and jumps; arrows, Enter, Esc; picking one centres the map on it (zooming in to at least a 40 px gate), keeps its route (`itMapPin`) and opens its tooltip. Shift+F10 / the ContextMenu key in the box opens the menu for the found system. A combobox with a listbox (aria).
4. **Right-click menu** on the map canvas (`contextmenu`): the system's name, Set destination (EVE's route), Set destination (Threat Vector's route) with "N jumps · ⚠ N flagged" under it, and Avoid this system / Stop avoiding. Focus on the first item, arrows move, Esc and a press outside close (it's a menu: no close button, CLAUDE.md "One close button"). Items off for your own system and for systems with no gate route. Inside the map box.
5. **Phone**: a press keeps the route and opens the tooltip (today); the tooltip then carries the three buttons (44 px) and takes presses.
6. **Setting the destination** (EVE login, a new permission "Waypoints", `esi-ui.write_waypoint.v1`, already registered on both apps):
   - SSO: `SSO_WP = 'esi-ui.write_waypoint.v1'`, `SSO_SCOPE_NAMES[SSO_WP] = 'Waypoints'`; asked for on the first press of either item (not logged in: log in first; the press is remembered and done on return, or a line says "Press again" — build review's choice); then kept in `ssoWant()` while the setting `tvRouteSend` (localStorage, set on the first successful send) is on, and shown on the profile's EVE tab with the other permissions (a lamp row like the others; off = forget it).
   - EVE's route: one `POST /ui/autopilot/waypoint?add_to_beginning=false&clear_other_waypoints=true&destination_id=<system>`.
   - Threat Vector's route: the first system after yours with `clear_other_waypoints=true`, then each next system with `clear_other_waypoints=false&add_to_beginning=false`, one request at a time, each waiting for the last 204, so they land in order. Stop on the first error and say which waypoint it reached.
   - Through `esiFetch` with the token (its request gate, the budget gauge's `ui` group from the `x-ratelimit-*` headers); a press while a send is running is refused with a line.
   - After a send, a note in the map box (one line, `role="status"`, its close button): "Destination set: X, N jumps", the warning if any, and "Every system is a waypoint: with Disable Autopilot at Each Waypoint on, the autopilot stops at each." For EVE's route: "EVE plans the route with your game's autopilot settings."
7. **Intel settings → Routes** box (after "Range and alerts"; not in the Intel example's numbered setup): Prefer shorter / Prefer safer / Prefer less secure (radios), security penalty 0–100 (the site's slider, off while Prefer shorter), avoid: gate camps, bubbles, systems with reported hostiles, neutrals, kills or ESS, systems on my avoid list; the list (each with Remove, like the wormhole list's), a field + Avoid (known systems only), one line: "For the map's routes and “Set destination (Threat Vector's route)”. EVE's route uses your game settings."
8. **Stored**: a new localStorage key `tvRoute` `{pref, pen, camp, bub, host, mine, list: [system ids]}` and `tvRouteSend`; nothing in `dscanIntel` changes (safe both ways: an older page ignores the new keys).

## What it flags (D5)
From `it.near`, every system Intel holds within "forget reports after": camp = `itGateTagHtml` gives a camp (a report's camp words, or 2+ kills at one gate); bubble = `itBubTagHtml`; reports (Q2) = a 'seen' report that isn't friendly-only ("hostiles" or "neutrals" by the side `itMapReports` would give it), a kill kept on the system (`st.kills`), or the ESS (`itEssTagHtml`: linked or at it); your list = `tvRoute.list`. Every system Intel holds, wherever it is (not limited to "Map: within"). One map index → reasons list, for the tooltip's words. Clear reports flag nothing.
